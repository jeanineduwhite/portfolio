Help and self-service

Expedia Group · Content Writer → Senior Content Designer → Content Manager · 2017–2022

What I owned over time: Traveler-facing help content, taxonomy and IA, voice/tone guidelines, and a migration of about 100 traveler-facing articles. Later I managed and mentored writers on the same kind of work.

I can’t share unreleased help or chatbot UI. This case is about how the content was structured so it could live in more than one place.

The shift

Help used to be search and browse. With chatbots and in-product self-service, the same answers have to work as a full article, a short suggested snippet, a chatbot reply, and sometimes a personalized signed-in view. If every article is a unique essay, none of that scales.

What I did

Wrote and maintained customer-facing help with an eye on findability: titles, taxonomy, and IA so people could get to the right article without knowing our internal names for things.

Migrated about 100 traveler-facing articles by rewriting and breaking them into components for classic help and chatbot answers. The unit of work stopped being “a page” and became “an answer you can reuse.”

Owned voice, tone, and style guidelines used across internal tools and customer-facing experiences, plus governance so distributed writers weren’t inventing parallel languages.

What I look for

  • The article title should match the way a person asks the question, not the name of the policy.
  • One article, one job. If it answers three problems, split it.
  • Components need a default order and rules for what the chatbot is allowed to skip.
  • Internal tools and customer help can share a voice without pretending they have the same reader.
