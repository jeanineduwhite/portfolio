Sail right through rainy days

WeatherPromise on Vrbo · Content Designer II · 2026

Paid add-on on eligible stays: if rain qualifies, the traveler gets a payout. The content has to make that feel simple without sounding like insurance, without promising Vrbo watches the weather, and without calling it free.

What I owned: product UI language across shopping (search, modules, Learn more, checkout) and the rules that kept Free and Paid from collapsing into one story. I also wrote the working content playbook so legal, design, and product were editing against the same constraints.

The line

Header Sail right through rainy days
Support WeatherPromise is an add-on that keeps the trip afloat and pays out if rain hits.

Personality lives in the header. The line under it does the actual work: it’s an add-on, the trip stays possible, money shows up as a payout if rain hits. That’s the whole offer in one breath.

What I wouldn’t let it say

An earlier direction was “Protect your trip from rainy days.” It sounds like a product headline. It also sounds like insurance. WeatherPromise is a third-party weather benefit, not a plan, policy, or coverage, so “protect” came out.

Same pass on money language. “Paid back” and “refund” read like Vrbo is returning the stay. The accurate word is payout, and only if they qualify.

On search, the filter stays even smaller:

Filter Stays eligible for a payout if it rains.

No “included,” no “free,” no mechanism. Campaign funding shows up later. Progressive disclosure: search gets eligibility; the module gets the sail line; Learn more gets the rain rule when we can be specific.

Trust, not cleverness

The product is easy to overclaim. I wouldn’t lead with “automatic payout.” I wouldn’t let Vrbo be the actor who monitors rain or sends money. The useful reassurance is on the traveler: you don’t have to track the rain — not “we watch the skies.”

When we couldn’t show the real threshold (days, hours, amount), I used a locked soft phrase instead of inventing a new weather metaphor. “If rain hits” / “if rain takes over your stay” is allowed. “If rain ruins your plans” is not, because it overpromises what qualifies.

What this shows

Writing for a benefit that sits next to legal. Systems: one vocabulary across Free and Paid. Judgment: when a punchy line is doing harm. This is the work I can’t fully screenshot, and the work I’m happiest talking through.
